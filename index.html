🌟 What Is code-doc-film?

code-doc-film is a small add-on for Claude Code, a tool that lets you talk to an AI assistant from your own computer. Once the add-on is in place, you describe a scene in plain English, for example "a short film about a volcano erupting at sunset", and the tool writes the code that draws that scene as a moving 3D film.

Nothing is filmed with a camera and nothing is downloaded from a stock video library. Every mountain, every cloud, every camera sweep and every note of background music is produced by the code itself. The result is a short documentary-style clip you can watch, share, or build on.

The project combines three ingredients that work quietly in the background: a 3D rendering engine called three.js, real elevation data so the landscapes look like actual places on Earth, and a small audio library called numpy that composes the soundtrack.

✨ What You Can Make With It

  • Landscape documentaries - sweeping shots across mountains, coastlines and valleys built from genuine terrain data.
  • Atmosphere and mood - fog, sunlight, dusk colours and slow drifting clouds that give a scene its feeling.
  • Original music - a soundtrack generated mathematically, so you never have to worry about licensing or copyright.
  • Camera movement - smooth fly-throughs and orbit shots that make a still landscape feel alive.
  • Repeatable recipes - describe the same scene twice and get a fresh render, ready for a series or a trailer.
  • Learning by watching - because the whole film is readable code, you can open it up and change one number to change the whole look.

🖥️ What You Need Before You Start

You do not need to be technical, but the film has to be drawn on your own machine, so a few ordinary things need to be in place:

  • A Windows 10 or Windows 11 computer.
  • An internet connection, used for the initial download and for fetching terrain data.
  • A modern web browser such as Edge, Chrome or Firefox, kept up to date. The films are drawn inside the browser.
  • Claude Code installed and signed in, since code-doc-film is a skill that plugs into it.
  • Roughly 500 MB of free disk space for the project files and rendered output.
  • A reasonably recent graphics card or built-in graphics chip. Anything from the last several years will manage a short clip.
A short film of 20 to 30 seconds is a comfortable starting point. Longer clips simply take a little more time to draw.

🎥 Make Your First Film in Four Steps

  1. Describe the scene. In Claude Code, ask for a short documentary film and describe what you want to see. Keep it to one or two sentences: a place, a time of day, and a mood.
  2. Let it build. The skill writes the scene, loads real terrain for the area you described, sets up the camera path and generates the music. This usually takes a short while.
  3. Watch it in your browser. When the build finishes, open the result in your browser. The film plays immediately, with no separate player to install.
  4. Adjust and repeat. Ask for a change in plain words, such as a slower camera or a darker sky, then rebuild. Each version is saved separately so you can keep the one you like.

🗺️ About the Real Terrain

The landscapes are not invented shapes. The skill pulls genuine elevation data for the region you name, so a film about the Alps really does show the Alps, and a film about a coastline follows the actual shape of that coast. Heights, ridges and valleys come straight from the data, which is why the results look grounded rather than cartoonish.

Because the terrain is fetched on demand, the first render of a new location takes slightly longer than a repeat of the same one. After that, the data sits in a local cache and later renders are quicker.

🎵 About the Generated Music

The soundtrack is composed with numpy, a maths library for sound. The skill chooses a key, a tempo and a small set of instrument-like tones that suit the mood you described, then lays them out across the length of the film. Nothing is borrowed from existing recordings, so there are no copyright concerns and no attribution needed when you share the result.

You can ask for a different feel at any time, for example something calmer, more tense, or sparse with long silences. The music is rebuilt alongside the scene each time.

🔧 Common Problems and Quick Fixes

The skill does not appear in Claude Code

The folder name matters. Open the project page again, check the exact name of the skill folder, and make sure your copy uses the same spelling. Then close Claude Code fully and reopen it.

The film will not load in the browser

Update your browser and try again. Older versions sometimes cannot draw the 3D scene. If you have a second browser installed, open the same file there to compare.

Rendering is slow or the picture stutters

Close other heavy programs while the film draws, and start with a shorter clip. A 15 to 20 second film is much lighter than a two minute one.

The landscape looks flat or plain

The terrain may not have finished loading. Rebuild the scene once with a stable internet connection, and check that the location you described is specific enough to look up.

There is no sound

Check your system volume and the volume inside the browser tab. Some browsers mute tabs automatically until you click on the page, so click once inside the film window.
